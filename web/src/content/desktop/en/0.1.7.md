- **The frame rate follows the screen.** New pictures go out as fast as the screen changes, up to the rate you set, and a screen that stays still is sent at about 10 a second until it moves again (a note under Details says so). Before, most new pictures were dropped and old ones repeated in their place, so a set 30 showed 20-something on the TV and a set 60 showed 40-something. The TV's frame counter now shows what the screen really does: 10 on a still screen, a movie's own 24 or 30.
- **H.265, when both ends can do it** (Settings → Video format): the receiver tests its H.265 decoder before it says it has one, and the computer needs a graphics card encoder (AMD or NVIDIA). H.264 stays the default. The casting page shows which one is in use.
- **Windows: casting a window sends that window's sound only** (a browser's included), not the music playing in another program. Switching to another window changes the sound without a click, and casting a whole screen now sounds as loud as casting a window did (the whole output used to come out about three times quieter).
- **Linux: windows can be chosen, and what is cast can be changed while casting.** The system's dialog offers windows as well as screens; **Choose again** on the home page asks again where a choice was remembered, and **Change what is cast…** on the casting page does it during a cast. A window is also the way round GNOME freezing the picture of a fullscreen video when a whole screen is cast.
