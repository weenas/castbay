- **The picture no longer gets worse over time on computers with an AMD graphics card.** The AMD encoder did not send the full pictures (key frames) that repair damage from a lost packet; a two-minute cast had one. CastBay now asks for one every 2 seconds, and a receiver that missed the first one (a TV waking from its screen saver, for example) picks the picture up again. The video library inside is updated to FFmpeg 9.0.2.
- **The tray menu** has **Settings**, **Switch what is cast** (the screens and windows, up to date while you cast, Windows only) and **About**; Stop casting is off while nothing is cast.
- **About is a window of its own**, with the version, whether a newer one is out, **What's new** and **Update**; the update runs there. Settings keeps one row for it, and the tray menu opens it too.
- **The update notice** has **Skip this version**; a newer version brings it back. A program left running (in the tray, started with the computer) now looks for a newer version again when its window is brought back, instead of going on with what it saw at start.
- **The window** opens in the middle of the screen, the title bar says the name once and in your language, and the top of the main page says the slogan.
- **The list of receivers**: a receiver that needs no pairing says **Open**; one this computer has paired with says only **Paired**, in green. The choice of what to cast reads **Entire screen / Single window**.
- **Linux**: the package is about 6 MB instead of 25 MB (the debug information is left out), and the settings and first-run words are about Linux, not Windows.
